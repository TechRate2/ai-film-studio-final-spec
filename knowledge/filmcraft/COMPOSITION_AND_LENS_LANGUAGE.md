# Composition and Lens Language

Evidence class: SYNTHESIS — reusable directing heuristics, not measured provider behavior.

Choose subject priority first. Scale controls information: a wide clarifies geography and body relationships; a medium preserves hands and expression; a close view isolates a decision/detail but loses context. Camera height changes access and perceived relation; low angle is not automatically powerful and high angle is not automatically weak. Compare what the viewer gains and loses with each framing. Use negative space only when expectation, off-screen presence or a graphic target needs it.

Perspective depends on camera position; lens feel and distance work together. For generative models use visible spatial relationships and depth rather than pretending a focal-length number guarantees optics. Deep focus may be essential for two planes of action; shallow focus may hide product proof or listener response. A rack focus should transfer attention to a story-relevant target, not merely advertise technique. Screen-safe framing accounts for subtitles/overlays and target crop without hiding important hands, faces or labels.

Movement must change information, relation or feeling. Pan follows a reveal, track maintains relation to action, push-in concentrates attention, pullback exposes isolation, orbit can shift allegiance/geography but adds continuity risk. Pick one dominant path with start/end framing; do not stack contradictory movement verbs. A locked camera is the baseline alternative. Preserve the intended axis and horizon unless disorientation is a motivated, brief choice followed by reorientation.
