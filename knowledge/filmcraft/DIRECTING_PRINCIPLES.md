# Directing Principles

Evidence class: SYNTHESIS — reusable directing heuristics, not measured provider behavior.

Start with audience perspective: whose experience are we sharing, what do they want, and what new perception must this beat create? Choose a single primary function (orient, reveal, prove, confront, react, release); let framing, acting and sound support it. A shot can sustain attention through accumulating meaning without movement. Remove a shot only after checking whether it carries orientation, breath or delayed comprehension that another shot cannot carry.

Direct actors with playable verbs such as reassure, conceal, test or persuade, a target and a change of tactic. “Be sad and cinematic” gives neither cause nor visible behavior. Convert subtext into a restrained observable action: a hand stops before touching a letter, an answer follows a withheld glance. Avoid requiring simultaneous tears, tremor, speech, walking, prop transfer and camera orbit if the route has not demonstrated that coordination. Choose the dominant action and preserve readable reaction time.

Before spend, compare the beat with an economical alternate: hold on the listener, use an existing insert, or reveal through sound. Do not simplify away the story's decisive action or factual proof just to make generation easy. Record purpose, chosen technique, rejected alternative, critical end state and acceptance cue in CreativeStrategy/UniversalVideoSpec. Review whether the viewer can explain what changed with sound off and whether the audio adds a distinct necessary layer; neither test is universal for sound-led work.

## Turn a genre request into a decision
Ask what the viewer must notice, believe or feel at this beat and what could prevent that. Retrieve a mechanism for that question, then adapt it to audience, brand, references and current generation reliability. A product demonstration, emotional reveal and factual explanation can share the same held-camera mechanism for different reasons. The same genre may need a still two-shot in one beat and a tracking reveal in the next. Never retrieve all niche cards or require every story to be Hook/Body/CTA when a quiet scene or open ending has another purpose.

Before finalizing, compare two economical staging/coverage alternatives using existing DecisionRecord fields: evidence gained/lost, performance burden, continuity risk, spoken-language/mouth requirement, total route cost and expected acceptance uncertainty. Select one plan, not multiple paid candidates by default. A creative critic should identify a concrete missing cause, unreadable action or broken promise and stop at the bounded critique budget; it cannot justify endless research or paid repair. Avoid ungrounded first-person customer testimony and do not use generated demonstrations as proof of an unverified product claim.
