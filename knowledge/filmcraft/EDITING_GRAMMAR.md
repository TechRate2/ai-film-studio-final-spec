# Editing Grammar

Evidence class: SYNTHESIS — reusable directing heuristics, not measured provider behavior.

Choose a cut because attention, emotion, information, time or location changes. Before cutting, ask what remains to be perceived in the current image. A reaction cut lands after comprehension; an anticipatory cut can build suspense. Match action preserves phase/direction/speed across viewpoints, while eyeline matching preserves perceived target and height. A jump or axis break can express discontinuity but needs intention and audience reorientation.

J-cut: incoming audio starts before its picture; L-cut: outgoing audio continues beneath the next picture. The audio clip therefore has its own source range and timeline start, not a forced video-length stem. Check intelligibility, accidental speaker overlap, room-tone transition and story knowledge. A bridge may imply continuity that the story does not permit; do not use it reflexively across a time jump. Reactions and silent holds may carry dialogue audio without buying extra visual generations if accepted coverage exists.

Montage compresses a progression with a changing variable (effort, time, relationship, evidence), not unrelated pretty shots. Alternate density and breath according to comprehension/emotion, not a fixed cut rate. Subtitle cues follow speech sense and readable rhythm; they need safe area and target-language review. Preflight source handles, frame/sample timebase, trims, speed and transition overlaps. Assembly executes the accepted timeline; it must not silently invent cuts, stretch speech, loop missing footage or conceal corrupt ranges.
