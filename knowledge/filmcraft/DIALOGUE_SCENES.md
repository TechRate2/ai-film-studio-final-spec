# Dialogue Scenes

Evidence class: SYNTHESIS — reusable directing heuristics, not measured provider behavior.

Plan the exchange as tactics, not alternating sentences: who seeks what from whom, what is withheld, which line changes the relationship, and who recognizes the change first? Check each line against that speaker's story-positioned knowledge and false beliefs. Give listening an objective. A delayed answer, interrupted gesture or gaze shift can be more economical than explanatory speech; specify only visible behavior the model can reasonably express.

Coverage follows power and information. A shared two-shot can sustain negotiation, a single can isolate a secret, and a listener close-up can show the consequence of an off-screen line. Over-shoulder foreground must not obscure the critical expression. Establish seating/standing geography and eyelines before reverses. Cut on a shift of intention or recognition, not mechanically on speaker changes. Avoid cutting away before the audience has registered the reaction; avoid holding merely to fill a provider duration.

For generative production allocate one clear speaking turn at a time unless overlap is essential and measured reliable. Native dialogue may reduce synchronization work but reduce voice/pronunciation control. Controlled post voice gives stable lines/identity but requires timing and, when mouths are visible, verified lip-sync compatibility. Off-screen dialogue or reaction coverage can be a legitimate creative route, not an automatic concealment of failure. A replacement voice must fit accepted timing; changing spoken words cannot be treated as a subtitle-only correction.
